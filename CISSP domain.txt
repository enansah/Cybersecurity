CISSP (Certified Information Systems Security Professional), which is a globally 
recognized professional standard and certification in cybersecurity. Think of CISSP 
as an official blueprint for the security world: it organizes the vast, complex 
field of protecting digital information into eight clear categories, called security 
domains.

Domains

1.Security and Risk Management
Security and risk management centers on establishing security goals, 
compliance (such as HIPAA regulations), business continuity, and risk mitigation.

2.Asset Security
Asset security focuses on safeguarding physical and digital assets, including the 
proper storage, retention, and secure destruction of data and hardware.

3.Security Architecture and Engineering 
Security architecture and engineering optimizes data security through effective 
tools and systems, such as configuring firewalls to filter traffic.

4.Communication and Network Security
Communication and network security involves managing and protecting physical networks 
and wireless environments, including setting policies against unsecured hotspots.

5.Identity and Access Management 
Identity and Access Management focuses on controlling and managing physical and logical 
assets by validating user identities and documenting access roles.

6.Security Assessment
Security Assessment and Testing involves conducting audits, control testing, and analyzing 
data to monitor for risks, threats, and unauthorized access.

7.Security Operations
Security Operations centers on conducting investigations, monitoring alerts, and carrying 
out preventative procedures against potential threats.

8.Software Development Security
Software Development Security applies secure coding practices and guidelines across the 
development life cycle to build safe applications.



Attack types

Password attack
A password attack is an attempt to access password-secured devices, systems, networks, or data.
Password attacks fall under the communication and network security domain. 

1.Brute force
2.Rainbow table


Social engineering attack
Social engineering is a manipulation technique that exploits human error to gain private 
information, access, or valuables.
Social engineering attacks are related to the security and risk management domain.

1. Phishing
2. Vishing
3. Business Email Compromise(BEC)
4. Water hole attack
etc


Physical attack
A physical attack is a security incident that affects not only digital but also physical 
environments where the incident is deployed. 
Physical attacks fall under the asset security domain. 

1.Malicious USB cable
2.Malicious flash drive
3.Card cloning and skimming


Adversarial artificial intelligence is a technique that manipulates 
technology to conduct attacks more efficiently.
Adversarial artificial intelligence falls under both the communication and network security 
and the identity and access management domains.



Supply-chain attack
A supply-chain attack targets systems, applications, hardware, and/or software to locate a 
vulnerability where malware can be deployed.
Supply-chain attacks can fall under several domains, including but not limited to the security 
and risk management, security architecture and engineering, and security operations domains.


Cryptographic attack
A cryptographic attack affects secure forms of communication between a sender and intended recipient. 
Cryptographic attacks fall under the communication and network security domain. 

1.Birthday
2.Collision
3.Downgrade




Threat actor types

1.Advanced persistent threats
Advanced persistent threats (APTs) have significant expertise accessing an organization's network 
without authorization.
APTs tend to research their targets (e.g., large corporations or government entities)  in advance 
and can remain undetected for an extended period of time. 

2.Insider threats
Insider threats abuse their authorized access to obtain data that may harm an organization.

3.Hacktivists
Hacktivists are threat actors that are driven by a political agenda. They abuse digital technology to 
accomplish their goals like social change campaigns, fame, demonstration and propaganda.

Hacker types
1.Authorized hackers are also called ethical hackers. They follow a code of ethics and adhere to the law 
to conduct organizational risk evaluations.
2.Semi-authorized hackers are considered researchers. They search for vulnerabilities but don’t take 
advantage of the vulnerabilities they find.
3.Unauthorized hackers are also called unethical hackers. They are malicious threat actors who do not follow 
or respect the law. Their goal is to collect and sell confidential data for financial gain.
